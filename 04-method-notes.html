4  Method notes

4.1 The estimand: declare the target

Target id Weights
V4-TARGET-RESPONDENT-WEIGHTED-v1 every respondent equally
V4-TARGET-EQUAL-CLUSTER-v1 every cluster equally

Under informative cluster size these are different population quantities. v4-estimand-and-sampling-contract-v1.yml requires the target to be declared, and every locked summary carries a target column so a number can never be read without one.

In the SWMDK application the two differ by 0.0024 on the teacher scale (0.6199 respondent-weighted, 0.6223 equal-cluster). Small — and reported separately, because the difference is a property of the population rather than noise to average away.

4.2 What is conditioned on, and what is resampled

From the estimand contract:

scale definition fixed
observed cluster ids fixed
observed cluster sizes conditioned
random weights a cluster-superpopulation posterior functional

Cluster sizes are conditioned on, not resampled. That is a modelling choice with consequences for what the interval covers, and it is written down rather than left implicit.

4.3 Exact H under arbitrary weights

The coefficient is computed by finite comonotone transport — exactly, under whatever weights a bootstrap draw produces, rather than by an approximation that happens to be close for equal weights.

NoteThe transport result is not claimed as new

Claim CL-03 in the frozen register requires the predecessor credit to Andreadis (2017) to be given, and the article never says “new transport theorem”. The contribution is the cluster-aware interval built on it, not the transport identity.

The independent oracle lane checks the implementation against a linear-programming solution of the same problem across an exhaustive grid: 32 configurations, 0 discrepancies.

4.4 The two-stage bootstrap and why calibration tightens

Resample clusters, then respondents within clusters. Before calibration this is conservative — the Phase 16 confirmation measured raw coverage at 99.75 % (respondent) and 99.63 % (equal-cluster) against a nominal 95 %.

So the correction narrows rather than widens: a factor k_G = 1 − 1.5/√G, which for the application’s G = 30 gives 0.7261.

Important

This runs the opposite way from the companion binary paper, whose calibration widens a slightly-short interval. Same idea — a measured correction to a measured miss — in opposite directions, because the two procedures err in opposite directions.

verify_semantics.R asserts the direction (p16_sel_raw_ratio < 1), and one of the negative controls flips it to confirm the check bites.

After calibration: 97.36 % and 97.38 % coverage, at about 75 % of the raw length. Still conservative relative to 95 %, and the paper says so rather than presenting it as exact.

4.5 Exact knots are disclosures, not errors

The transport path reports exact-knot counts — configurations where a marginal CDF gap is exactly zero. The article reports these as diagnostic disclosures of the weighted-marginal transport path, not error flags, and the application had 2 on the teacher respondent-weighted lane and 0 on the equal-cluster lane.

They matter for reading the archival defect: the corrected kernel advances on exact zero by design, so an exact knot is a handled case rather than a risk.

4.6 The claim boundary

Six phrases the article never asserts outside a negation:

“first-ever” · “universally superior” · “new transport theorem” · “credible interval” · “posterior probabilit…” · “analytically derived”

The interval is a confidence interval. The hierarchical Bayesian bootstrap is a resampling device, and the property claimed is repeated-sampling coverage.

A frozen claim-eligibility register of 14 claims, written at the source-review gate, governs the article’s wording. It ships as Table H1; verify_semantics.R asserts all 14 are transcribed into it.
