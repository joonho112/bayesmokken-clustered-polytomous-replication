1  The claim, in one page

Mokken’s scalability coefficient H is normally computed as if respondents were independent. They often are not: pupils sit in classrooms, patients in clinics, respondents in interviewer caseloads. This paper supplies an interval for polytomous H that accounts for that clustering, and whose coverage has been measured rather than assumed.

1.1 Two population targets, not one estimator

The distinction the whole paper turns on, and the first thing the design fixes:

Target Means
respondent-weighted every respondent counts equally
equal-cluster every cluster counts equally

Under informative cluster size — when a cluster’s size is associated with its scalability — these are different population quantities, not two estimators of one thing. Reporting “the” H without saying which is a category error, so the estimand contract requires the target to be declared, never inferred, and every locked summary carries a target column.

1.2 The method

  1. A two-stage hierarchical Bayesian bootstrap: resample clusters, then respondents within clusters, under a declared weight law.
  2. Compute H exactly under arbitrary weights by finite comonotone transport — not by an approximation that happens to be close.
  3. Tighten the resulting interval by a factor depending on the number of clusters G.

Step 3 runs the opposite way from the companion binary paper, and that is not an accident. The two-stage bootstrap is conservative before calibration — it over-covers — so the correction narrows rather than widens.

1.3 What the confirmation found

A fresh 24-cell confirmation at 6,400 replications per target, run after the calibration was frozen:

Raw Calibrated
respondent-weighted coverage 99.75 % 97.36 %
equal-cluster coverage 99.63 % 97.38 %
respondent-weighted mean length 0.1167 0.0877

The raw interval over-covers badly at ~99.7 %. The calibrated one lands near 97.4 % — still conservative relative to 95 %, and about 25 % shorter (length ratio 0.751).

1.4 The application

SWMDK: 639 pupils in 30 classrooms, cluster sizes 5–29, two prespecified six- and five-item scales.

Teacher scale Value
respondent-weighted H 0.6199
equal-cluster H 0.6223
respondent-weighted 95 % CI [0.5728, 0.6692]
equal-cluster 95 % CI [0.5743, 0.6698]
width multiplier 0.7261

The two targets differ by 0.0024 here — small, but reported separately because the difference is a property of the population, not noise to be averaged away.

12 tasks × 99,999 draws = 1,199,988 draws, with zero invalid draws.

1.5 The boundary

This is a confidence interval, not a posterior credible interval. The hierarchical Bayesian bootstrap is a resampling device; the property claimed is repeated-sampling coverage.

Six phrases the article deliberately never asserts:

“first-ever” · “universally superior” · “new transport theorem” · “credible interval” · “posterior probabilit…” · “analytically derived”

Each occurs in the manuscript only inside a negation or boundary statement, and a frozen claim-eligibility register — 14 claims, written at the source-review gate — governs the article’s wording. It ships as Table H1, and verify_semantics.R asserts all 14 are transcribed.

The comonotone transport result is not claimed as new: CL-03 in that register requires the predecessor credit to Andreadis (2017) to be given.
